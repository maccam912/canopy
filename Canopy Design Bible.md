# Canopy: Design Bible

Nov 16, 2026 · @Matthew Koski

## Pitch

Canopy is a side-view terrarium where plants evolve on their own and you play the climate. Leaves in light earn growth, wood has weight, and everything else follows from those two rules.

You drag the sun, snip, prop and wait. The forest leans, overreaches and comes down in slow, creaking cascades, then grows back different.

It is a toy first: no score, no fail state, and a session can last five minutes or an hour. The tone is slow zen chaos, meaning long quiet build-ups, one touch, and an unhurried collapse.

## Pillars

Every feature is checked against these six. If it breaks one, it needs a very good reason.

1. **Two rules.** Light feeds and weight breaks. Anything that needs a third rule has to earn it.
2. **You are the climate, not the gardener.** You never design a plant. You set conditions and the forest answers.
3. **New and spectacular beat efficient.** The game notices firsts and big falls, never yield per minute.
4. **Slow build, slow fall.** Tension gathers over minutes, and the release takes its time too.
5. **Read it off the shape.** There are no stat panels. A plant's habits show in its silhouette and its history in its rings.
6. **Nothing is lost.** A wipeout leaves rich soil, so every ending is a start.

## The world

The world is one glass jar seen from the side, with a soil floor, a sun on an arc, and two rules.

**Light feeds.** Light travels in straight rays from the sun. Each leaf a ray passes through takes a share and dims what lies behind it. Shade is therefore soft and layered, and a plant under three crowns gets a trickle, not nothing.

**Weight breaks.** Every piece of wood has mass, stiffness and a breaking point. Trunks sag, lean and sway, and they snap or uproot when overloaded. Fallen wood stays in the world as an object that knocks, pins, bridges and props.

Three supporting details complete the world:

- **Soil** has one property, richness. Roots drain it, and it seeps slowly sideways to even out.
- **Rot** turns fallen wood and leaves into richness where they lie.
- **Water** raises richness and softens the ground, so roots grip less after rain.

The floor has gentle terrain, with hollows that collect water and ledges that catch low sun. This gives different spots different conditions without adding rules.

## The plants

Each plant grows from a seed that carries eight inherited habits. The player never sees them as numbers, only as shape and behavior.

| Habit | What it sets | What you see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach | How hard new growth bends toward light | Straight poles or curling sun-chasers |
| Fork | How often and how wide branches split | Spires, bushes or umbrellas |
| Wood | Thin and fast, or thick and slow | Whippy stems or stout trunks |
| Leaf | A few big leaves, or many small ones | Broad shade-casters or feathery crowns |
| Root | How much growth goes underground | Deep anchors or shallow spreaders |
| Seed | Many light seeds, or a few heavy ones | Drifting clouds or plump droppers |
| Patience | How big it gets before it starts seeding | Early weeds or late giants |
| Thrift | How little light it can idle on | Understory lurkers or hungry sprinters |

**Life.** Light earned at the leaves flows down the stem. Every piece of wood takes its upkeep first, and what remains goes to tips, roots and seeds. A plant dies when it cannot pay its upkeep. There is no age limit, but big bodies cost more to run.

**Seeds.** A seed copies its parent's habits with small random nudges. Seeds travel three ways: they drop, they drift on wind, or they are flung when the parent falls. A taller, faster fall throws them farther.

**Shapes.** The game groups living plants by how alike their habits are. When a group drifts far enough from every known one, it becomes a new shape and gets a name in the field guide. Leaf color follows lineage, so relatives look alike at a glance.

## Runaway loops

Four loops push the forest toward excess, and three of them run through a fall.

Growth leads to a fall, and each fall returns light, soil and momentum to the forest.

- **Race.** Shade starves the short, so everything bolts upward until something buckles.
- **Gap.** A fallen giant opens a hole of light, and the floor under it erupts in saplings.
- **Dominoes.** Trunks lean toward the sun. A fast swing unbalances a whole stand, and one fall takes out a row.
- **Rot.** Dead wood feeds the soil, so crash sites become the richest ground and get crashed on again.

Three brakes stop the loops from running forever. Shade starves the losers, upkeep taxes the giants, and roots drain the soil they stand in. Tuning the game mostly means tuning how hard the loops push against these brakes.

## Expected strategies

These eight shapes are predictions of what the rules should produce, not content to be authored. The prototype decides which ones are real.

| Shape | Its bet | Thrives when | Dies when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pole | All height on thin wood | Crowded, calm, sun parked | Wind blows or the sun swings |
| Umbrella | A wide flat crown that shades everything | Open ground, sun overhead | Sun sits low, or a neighbor grows taller |
| Lurker | Squat and thrifty, waits in shade | Under a closed canopy, then bursts into gaps | Left in open sun and outgrown |
| Vine | No wood of its own, leans on neighbors | Dense stands of stiff trunks | Its host falls |
| Toppler | Flimsy on purpose, falls to fling seeds | Open ground lies downrange | Packed in with nowhere to land |
| Chaser | Bendy with strong reach | The sun moves often | The sun parks and rigid plants win |
| Anchor | Deep roots and a thick base | Storms and soft wet ground | Racing from a slow start |
| Weed | Tiny, seeds early and often | Bare soil after a wipeout | Anything taller arrives |

The Vine and the Toppler matter most. Both come free from the physics, and both would show that the forest is inventing strategies nobody scripted.

## Your hands

You get seven tools, all direct touch, and each arrives after an event in the forest instead of being bought.

| Tool | What it does | Arrives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sun | Drag it along its arc, park it, or keep it moving | From the start |
| Snip | Cut any stem. The cut part falls as dead wood | From the start |
| Seed jar | Pocket a seed from any plant and start it anywhere | When the first plant seeds |
| Prop | Wedge a stick under a leaning trunk | After the first fall |
| Water | Pour rain to enrich and soften the soil | When the first log rots away |
| Tie | Run a string between two stems | After the first fall of three |
| Wind | Blow across the jar from either side | After the first fall of five |

Two limits keep you in the role of climate. You cannot edit a plant's habits, and you cannot place a plant except as a seed.

## Play structure

Six layers sit on top of the simulation. Each one rewards novelty or spectacle, and none rewards efficiency.

### Build-up and release

The core rhythm is minutes of quiet growth followed by one touch and a long fall. Large trunks come down slowly, and each sounds a note set by its length. A domino run therefore plays as a slow, creaking phrase.

### Field guide

Every new shape gets a generated name and a pressed leaf on its own page. The guide also logs firsts, such as the first vine, the first plant to survive a fall, and the first to touch the lid. Personal records live here as notes, not targets.

### Visitors

Small creatures move in when the forest offers what they need. Each one nudges a loop and none can be controlled.

| Visitor | Needs | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fireflies | A fresh gap at dusk | Gather where light reaches the floor |
| Beetles | Rotting wood | Speed up rot where they settle |
| Birds | A tall standing crown | Carry heavy seeds across the jar |
| Squirrel | Plants with plump seeds | Buries seeds, and some sprout far away |
| Owl | A trunk that has stood for five generations | Leaves just before its tree falls |

### Wishes

One optional prompt is pinned at a time, and it can be skipped freely. A wish can only be met indirectly, by setting up a climate that gets there. Examples:

- A fall that takes down ten.
- Five shapes alive at once.
- A plant that touches the lid.
- A vine that outlives its host.
- A forest where nothing grows taller than your thumb.

A finished wish earns a stamp in the field guide and nothing else.

### The jar grows

When a plant touches the lid and holds, the glass cracks and a bigger home opens: jar, then tank, then greenhouse, then hillside. Old jars stay on the shelf and keep growing. The seed jar moves seeds between them.

### Away time

The forest keeps growing while the game is closed, up to a cap. On return, a short journal lists the headline events. Any stump can be cut to read the plant's life in its rings, wide for bright years and narrow for shaded ones.

## A 20-minute session

This is the experience to aim for, written as a returning player in a mid-game tank. It is a target, not a recording.

| Minute | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | You open the tank. The journal reports two falls overnight and one new shape. Poles wall off the left side, and Lurkers crowd under a rotting log on the right. |
| 1 | The field guide shows the new shape: a pale vine riding the Poles, named Hanging Thread. |
| 2 | The pinned wish reads "A fall that takes down ten." Eleven Poles stand in a row, so you decide to set it up. |
| 3 | You park the sun low on the right and fast-forward. The Poles begin bending toward it. |
| 6 | Crowns now hang past their own roots. The vines thicken and add weight up high. The first creaks start. |
| 8 | One Pole snaps too early and takes two neighbors with it. Three are down, not ten, and a gap opens mid-row. |
| 9 | You prop the next Pole so the rest of the row holds. Lurkers surge into the gap. You pour water at the left end to soften the ground. |
| 12 | The row has filled back in, heavier and more tilted. The owl leaves its trunk, which means something is close. |
| 13 | You drop to normal speed and make one snip at the base of the leftmost Pole. |
| 13 to 14 | The fall takes forty seconds. Each trunk comes down slowly and sounds its note. The vines drag two extra plants along. The propped Pole holds for a beat, then goes. Twelve are down and the wish is stamped. |
| 15 | Light floods the floor and hundreds of sprouts appear. Beetles arrive on the fallen wood. |
| 16 | Many of the new Poles are bendier than their parents, because the stiff ones were the ones that snapped. You pocket a seed from a survivor. |
| 18 | You swing the sun slowly back and forth. The bendy sprouts follow it. A new shape may be forming, but it has no name yet. |
| 20 | You park the sun overhead and close the tank. It keeps growing. |

The early snap at minute 8 is the important beat. A failed setup still produces a fall, a gap and a new generation, so there is no losing move.

## Look, sound and pace

The presentation has one job, which is to make light, strain and ancestry visible without any interface.

**Look.** The jar is backlit glass with light falling in visible shafts. Plants are simple strokes whose width is the wood's thickness. Pulses of glow travel down a stem as energy flows, so you can see which plants are earning and which are starving.

**Sound.** There is no music track, because the forest is the instrument. Creaks rise in pitch as strain builds. Each falling trunk sounds one note, low for long trunks and high for short ones. Rain, wind and insects fill the quiet.

**Pace.** These are starting targets to tune in the prototype:

| Setting | Target |
| --- | --- |
| One plant generation at normal speed | 1 to 3 minutes |
| Fast-forward | Up to 8x |
| A large trunk coming down | 3 to 5 seconds |
| Speed during a big fall | Always drops to normal |

**Touch.** One finger does everything. Pinch zooms and holding fast-forwards.

## What Canopy leaves out

These are deliberate omissions, and each protects a pillar.

- **No score, currency, timers or fail states.** They turn a playground into a job.
- **No plant designer or habit editor.** Designing a plant would make you the gardener.
- **No tech tree or building.** Tools arrive from events in the forest.
- **No streaks or penalties for being away.** Away time only ever adds.
- **No second ecosystem.** Visitors nudge loops, but there are no herbivores or predators to manage.
- **No 3D.** The side view keeps light and shade readable.

## Risks and open questions

Monoculture is the biggest risk, and none of the answers below has been tested yet.

| Risk | Why it matters | Planned answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monoculture | One shape wins and the forest looks the same forever | The player is the disturbance. Storms and terrain back up a passive player. Visitors and wishes reward variety |
| Dead time between falls | Zen tips over into boring | Fast-forward, visible strain, the owl's warning, growth while away |
| Evolution at the wrong speed | Too slow to notice, or too fast to follow | Tune nudge size and generation time. Lineage colors and field guide alerts make change visible |
| Degenerate winners | A carpet of weeds that never grows up, or one tower that never dies | Upkeep rises with size and shade kills carpets. Both need proof in the prototype |
| Too indirect | Wishes feel like luck, not play | Snip, prop, tie and the seed jar give direct control over the release |
| Hard to read | You cannot tell why a plant died | Stem glow, tree rings, soft visible shade |
| Physics cost | Hundreds of jointed stems on a phone | 2D only, old wood fuses rigid, idle bodies sleep, segments are capped |

Open questions:

- [ ] Does water need to be more than richness plus soft ground?
- [ ] Should plants cross-breed, or is copying with nudges enough?
- [ ] Do seasons add anything a draggable sun does not?
- [ ] How should shapes be grouped and named so a name feels earned?
- [ ] Is one phone screen wide enough for a ten-trunk cascade?
- [ ] Which platform comes first, touch or desktop?

## First prototype

The first build answers one question: do the two rules alone produce falls worth watching and shapes that differ?

**In scope**

- One jar with flat soil and a draggable sun
- Light rays with layered shade
- Stem physics with sagging, snapping and uprooting
- Five habits: Reach, Fork, Wood, Leaf and Seed
- Seeds that drop and are flung by falls
- Rot that feeds the soil
- Snip and fast-forward

**Out of scope**

Field guide, visitors, wishes, the growing jar, away time, water, wind, and all sound beyond a single fall note.

**It passes if**, after an hour running unattended and twenty minutes of poking:

- [ ] At least three visibly different shapes are alive at the same time at some point.
- [ ] A parked sun and a swinging sun grow visibly different forests.
- [ ] One snip in a mature stand can bring down five or more plants.
- [ ] A fall is satisfying to watch with the sound off.

If any check fails, the rules get fixed before any feature is added.
